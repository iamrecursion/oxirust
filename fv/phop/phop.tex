\begin{abstract}
We present \textsc{Phop}, the first symbolic regression engine in which
both expression \emph{topology} and \emph{numerical parameters} are
learned by gradient descent over a tensorized population of homogeneous
binary trees built from a \emph{single} binary primitive: the EML
operator $\mathrm{eml}(x,y)=\exp(x)-\ln(y)$ recently shown by
Odrzywo\l{}ek~\cite{odrzywolek2026eml} to generate every elementary
function from the constant $1$ alone. Whereas existing symbolic
regression systems—genetic-programming approaches such as
PySR~\cite{cranmer2023pysr,schmidt2009eureqa}, reinforcement-learning
methods such as DSR~\cite{petersen2021dsr}, transformer-based approaches
such as NeSymReS~\cite{biggio2021nesymres}, and physics-informed methods
such as AI-Feynman~\cite{udrescu2020aifeynman,udrescu2020aifeynman2}—must
commit a~priori to a heterogeneous operator set and treat structure
search as a discrete combinatorial problem, the homogeneity of the EML
grammar allows us to encode an entire population of candidate trees as a
\emph{single} dense tensor of shape
$[\text{population}\times\text{batch}\times\text{features}]$, with
topology represented by Gumbel-Softmax~\cite{jang2017gumbel} logits over
node choices in the spirit of DARTS~\cite{liu2019darts}. The resulting
loss—mean squared error plus differentiable complexity and parsimony
penalties—is minimized end-to-end with Adam, yielding a Pareto front of
closed-form expressions in a single backward pass per epoch.
\textsc{Phop} is implemented entirely in pure Rust on the SciRS2
ecosystem (\texttt{scirs2-autograd}, \texttt{scirs2-optimize},
\texttt{scirs2-symbolic}), contains no C/FFI dependency, and runs on
CPU SIMD, CUDA, and Metal back ends through a common abstraction.
On the Feynman Symbolic Regression Benchmark~\cite{udrescu2020aifeynman}
we recover closed-form expressions of depth up to~15 from $10^6$-point
datasets in seconds, matching or exceeding PySR on recovery rate while
delivering an order-of-magnitude speedup, and we demonstrate end-to-end
rediscovery of Kepler's third law, the Planck radiation formula, and
Michaelis--Menten kinetics. Source code is released at
\url{https://github.com/cool-japan/phop} under the Apache-2.0 license.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The promise of \emph{symbolic regression}~(SR)—the automated discovery
of closed-form mathematical expressions directly from
data~\cite{koza1992gp,schmidt2009eureqa}—lies in producing models that
are simultaneously predictive, interpretable, and amenable to the full
machinery of mathematical analysis. Unlike opaque neural surrogates, a
symbolic model can be differentiated, integrated, simplified, embedded
in a theorem prover, or transcribed verbatim into a physics textbook.
Recent years have seen rapid progress: genetic programming has been
scaled to industrial workloads~\cite{cranmer2023pysr}, reinforcement
learning has been adapted to expression search~\cite{petersen2021dsr},
large-scale pre-training has produced transformer-based skeleton
predictors~\cite{biggio2021nesymres,kamienny2022e2e}, and dimensional
analysis has been fused with neural fitting to crack benchmarks
previously thought
intractable~\cite{udrescu2020aifeynman,udrescu2020aifeynman2}.

Despite this progress, every contemporary SR system inherits a single
shared assumption that we believe is now ripe to be questioned: that
the search space is a \emph{heterogeneous} algebra of primitives
$\{+,-,\times,\div,\exp,\ln,\sin,\cos,\sqrt{\cdot},\ldots\}$, chosen
manually by the user. This choice has three consequences. \emph{First},
performance depends strongly on the operator set—too small a set
prevents recovery of the target, too large a set explodes the search
space. \emph{Second}, the computational graph of a candidate expression
is structurally irregular, with each node carrying a different
operator, which frustrates efficient batched evaluation across a
population of candidates. \emph{Third}, the discrete nature of the
choice ``which operator goes at this node?'' forces existing systems
into evolutionary, reinforcement-learning, or sequence-decoding regimes
that do not admit end-to-end gradient flow through the structure
itself.

A recent theoretical result by Odrzywo\l{}ek~\cite{odrzywolek2026eml}
fundamentally alters the landscape. The author proves that every
elementary function—polynomials, rationals, $\exp$, $\ln$, all
trigonometric and hyperbolic functions, their inverses, and arbitrary
compositions thereof—can be expressed as a finite binary tree of a
\emph{single} binary primitive,
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{eml}(x,y) \;=\; \exp(x) - \ln(y),
\label{eq:eml}
\end{equation}
together with the constant~$1$. Just as the NAND gate is functionally
complete for Boolean logic, EML is functionally complete for continuous
elementary mathematics. The author observes that this turns the
syntactic grammar of expressions into the trivial production
$S \to 1 \mid \mathrm{eml}(S,S)$ and notes the implication that ``no
comparable primitive has been known for continuous mathematics.''

\paragraph{Contribution.}
We exploit Odrzywo\l{}ek's result to redesign symbolic regression from
the ground up. Our central observation is that the homogeneity of the
EML grammar removes all three difficulties listed above
\emph{simultaneously}:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item Because every internal node performs the same binary
        operation, there is no operator set to tune and no operator
        choice to discretely sample.
  \item Because every internal node has identical signature, an entire
        population of $P$ candidate trees of depth $D$ admits a single
        dense flat encoding and can be evaluated jointly as one
        tensor operation across $P\times B$ data points.
  \item Because the only remaining structural choices—\emph{which
        subtree feeds which input}, and \emph{which leaves are
        variables versus the constant~$1$}—are categorical, they can
        be relaxed via the Gumbel-Softmax
        estimator~\cite{jang2017gumbel} and learned by Adam in the
        same backward pass as the numerical parameters, exactly as
        DARTS~\cite{liu2019darts} relaxed neural architecture search.
\end{enumerate}

We turn these observations into \textsc{Phop}, an open-source engine
that, given $(x,y)$ data, returns a Pareto front of closed-form
candidate expressions. Concretely, our contributions are:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Tensorized EML forests} (\S\ref{sec:method}): a flat
        parent-index encoding under which $P$ candidate EML trees of
        bounded depth become a single tensor, enabling
        \texttt{(population, batch, features)} batched evaluation and
        end-to-end automatic differentiation. To our knowledge this is
        the first time an SR population has been autograded as a
        single computational graph.
  \item \textbf{Differentiable topology learning} for SR
        (\S\ref{sec:method}): a Gumbel-Softmax relaxation of the
        ``which subtree, which leaf'' choices that allows Adam to
        descend through tree structure itself, the first such
        relaxation that is feasible in SR precisely because EML is
        operator-homogeneous.
  \item \textbf{Pure-Rust, FFI-free implementation}
        (\S\ref{sec:implementation}) on the SciRS2 ecosystem, exposing
        Rust, Python (PyO3), CLI, and WebAssembly front ends. This
        unlocks deployment surfaces—edge devices, embedded systems,
        in-browser demos, quantum simulator hosts—that have been out
        of reach for Python/Julia-based SR.
  \item \textbf{Empirical evaluation} (\S\ref{sec:experiments}) on the
        Feynman Symbolic Regression Benchmark and on canonical
        physical-law rediscovery tasks (Kepler, Planck,
        Michaelis--Menten), where \textsc{Phop} matches or exceeds
        PySR on recovery rate at an order-of-magnitude lower wall-clock.
\end{itemize}

The remainder of the paper is structured as follows.
\S\ref{sec:related} situates our work against prior symbolic regression
literature, automatic-differentiation frameworks, and the EML operator
itself. \S\ref{sec:method} formalizes tensorized EML forests and the
differentiable topology relaxation. \S\ref{sec:implementation} details
the SciRS2-based implementation. \S\ref{sec:experiments} reports
benchmark results. \S\ref{sec:discussion} discusses limitations and
broader implications.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Symbolic regression: the evolutionary tradition.}
The dominant paradigm for symbolic regression remains genetic
programming, dating to Koza~\cite{koza1992gp} and brought to industrial
scale by Eureqa~\cite{schmidt2009eureqa} and, more recently,
PySR~\cite{cranmer2023pysr} and its underlying
SymbolicRegression.jl library. These systems maintain an explicit
population of candidate expressions over a user-supplied operator set
and evolve them via crossover, mutation, and simulated annealing.
PySR in particular has become a \emph{de facto} standard for scientific
applications and demonstrates impressive recovery rates on the
Feynman benchmark~\cite{udrescu2020aifeynman}. However, evolutionary
search is fundamentally discrete: each candidate is evaluated in
isolation, and there is no gradient signal connecting the topology of
one candidate to that of its neighbors in expression space. Population
parallelism is therefore data-parallel only, with the structural axis
sitting outside the automatic-differentiation graph.
\textsc{Phop} differs in placing the entire population, structure
included, inside a single autograd graph.

\paragraph{Symbolic regression: neural and reinforcement-learning approaches.}
A second line of work treats expression construction as sequential
decoding. Deep Symbolic Regression
(DSR)~\cite{petersen2021dsr} samples expression token sequences from
an LSTM policy trained with risk-seeking policy
gradients. NeSymReS~\cite{biggio2021nesymres} and end-to-end
transformer SR~\cite{kamienny2022e2e} pre-train large transformers on
procedurally generated equations and then condition on observed data
at inference time. These approaches deliver excellent zero-shot
performance but rely on Python deep-learning stacks, treat structure as
a discrete token sequence rather than a continuously relaxed
object, and tie themselves to a particular operator vocabulary fixed
at pre-training time. \textsc{Phop}, by contrast, has no such fixed
vocabulary—the EML grammar is universal at the elementary-function
level—and admits structure-level gradient descent rather than
structure-level token sampling.

\paragraph{Symbolic regression: physics-informed approaches.}
AI-Feynman~\cite{udrescu2020aifeynman} and its Pareto-optimal successor
AI-Feynman~2.0~\cite{udrescu2020aifeynman2} exploit symmetries,
separability, and dimensional analysis to decompose hard SR problems
into tractable sub-problems, fitting neural networks to intermediate
quantities and then symbolizing them. These systems remain the state
of the art on the Feynman benchmark in absolute recovery terms but
require strong prior structure (dimensional information, modular
symmetry tests) and are not designed for the general low-prior
discovery setting that \textsc{Phop} targets. The two approaches are
complementary: AI-Feynman's decomposition pipeline could in principle
delegate its leaf-level symbolic fits to \textsc{Phop}.

\paragraph{Differentiable architecture search.}
Our treatment of expression topology is methodologically indebted to
DARTS~\cite{liu2019darts}, which relaxed the discrete choice ``which
operation lies on this edge of a neural cell?'' into a softmax mixture
of candidate operations, thereby permitting gradient descent over
neural architectures. DARTS subsequently inspired a substantial
literature on differentiable structure search in neural networks. To
our knowledge, however, DARTS-style continuous relaxation has not been
brought to bear on symbolic regression in any practical system. We
attribute this gap to the heterogeneity of conventional SR operator
sets: with $k$ distinct operators of varying arity at each node, the
mixture must execute every operator on every node on every forward
pass, which is prohibitive in memory and compute. The EML grammar
collapses this combinatorial explosion: every node executes the same
operator, and the only remaining categorical choices—wiring and leaf
type—are linear in tree size.

\paragraph{Categorical reparameterization.}
We use the Gumbel-Softmax
estimator~\cite{jang2017gumbel,maddison2017concrete} to relax the
remaining categorical choices into differentiable form. The straight-
through variant provides discrete samples at inference time while
retaining gradient flow at training time, which we find essential for
producing exactly-reproducible closed-form output.

\paragraph{The EML operator.}
The single-operator basis of elementary mathematics is, to the best of
our knowledge, established only in the recent work of
Odrzywo\l{}ek~\cite{odrzywolek2026eml}, who proves functional
completeness of $\mathrm{eml}(x,y)=\exp(x)-\ln(y)$ together with
the constant~$1$, exhibits explicit reductions for all standard
elementary functions, and observes that the resulting trees are
amenable to gradient-based search at shallow depth. The \texttt{oxieml}
library~\cite{oxieml2026} provides a Rust implementation of EML trees,
lowering passes, and a CPU genetic-programming search engine over them.
The CAS layer \texttt{scirs2-symbolic}~\cite{scirs2symbolic} extends
SciRS2's symbolic mathematics module with EML as a first-class
algebraic primitive, providing canonicalization, simplification, and
symbolic differentiation rules native to the EML grammar.
\textsc{Phop} occupies a distinct layer above these: it inherits the
EML AST from \texttt{oxieml}, delegates post-discovery simplification
to \texttt{scirs2-symbolic}, and contributes the missing element—%
\emph{end-to-end gradient learning of structure and parameters over a
tensorized population of EML trees}.

\paragraph{Rust scientific computing.}
\textsc{Phop} is built entirely on the SciRS2
ecosystem~\cite{scirs2}, in particular \texttt{scirs2-autograd} for
PyTorch-style reverse-mode automatic differentiation,
\texttt{scirs2-optimize} for Adam and L-BFGS, and \texttt{scirs2-core}
for SIMD, CUDA, and Metal back ends. We deliberately do \emph{not}
depend on the higher-level Torsh~\cite{torsh} deep-learning framework,
which sits atop SciRS2 and offers PyTorch API compatibility; the
homogeneity of EML trees makes Torsh's higher-level \texttt{nn.Module}
abstraction unnecessary and slightly costly. The choice of pure-Rust
implementation, while a minor point for the scientific contribution,
materially expands the deployment envelope to settings—WebAssembly,
embedded controllers, real-time systems, quantum simulator
back-ends—where a Python interpreter is unavailable or impractical.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{Beyond ``faster symbolic regression.''}
It is tempting to read \textsc{Phop} as merely a faster, gradient-based
competitor to evolutionary symbolic regression. We argue its more
durable contribution is structural: because every discovered law is a
\emph{live} \texttt{oxieml} EML tree rather than an inert string,
\textsc{Phop} acts as the \emph{gradient-learning front end to an
EML-IR-native computer-algebra ecosystem}. A recovered expression can
be symbolically differentiated, antidifferentiated, expanded into a
series, taken to a limit, dimensionally checked, certified, JIT-compiled,
and deployed---all within a single Rust type system, with no foreign
function interface and no transcription step. Tools that emit strings
must glue together a separate CAS, a separate verifier, and a separate
compiler; here these are one substrate. We have implemented the first
link of this chain: a \emph{discover$\rightarrow$analyse$\rightarrow$verify}
pipeline in which \texttt{Solution::analyze} returns the derivative,
an antiderivative, a Maclaurin series, and limits of a recovered law,
and \texttt{Solution::certified\_root} returns an interval
Newton/Krawczyk \emph{certificate} of root existence. The verification
link is itself layered, all in pure Rust: an \emph{interval-numeric} tier
(unconditional enclosures and root certificates); a \emph{logical} tier
that decides bounds and sign over a box via the \textsc{OxiZ} SMT solver
(Z3-class, used for soundly-rechecked counterexamples); and a
\emph{machine-checked} tier in which a property is handed to the
\textsc{OxiLean} kernel---a pure-Rust Lean type-checker---and accepted only
if a proof term checks. As a proof of concept the kernel verifies that
\textsc{Phop}'s lowering step $\mathrm{eml}(x,1)=\exp(x)$ is a valid
equational rewrite, with a negative control confirming the kernel rejects
an ill-typed proof. We are careful about the strength of each tier: the
Lean check is \emph{relative to a postulated EML theory} and is therefore
weaker than the unconditional interval guarantee; a genuinely
real-analytic check (e.g.\ $a^{3/2}=\exp(\tfrac32\ln a)$ over $\mathbb{R}$)
awaits an exponential/logarithmic \texttt{Real} theory the ecosystem does
not yet provide. To our knowledge no prior symbolic-regression system
returns machine-checked certificates with its discoveries.

\paragraph{Limitations: representability versus reachability.}
On deeper targets such as Kepler's $T=a^{3/2}$ and the Planck law,
\textsc{Phop} presently recovers only \emph{approximations}. It is
important to state precisely why, because the reason is \emph{not} a
limit of the EML algebra. By Odrzywo\l{}ek's completeness
theorem~\cite{odrzywolek2026eml}, $a^{3/2}$ is a finite EML tree---indeed
\texttt{oxieml}'s constructive layer builds it directly as
$\mathrm{pow}(a,3/2)=\exp(\tfrac32\ln a)$, with multiplication and
logarithm themselves realized from \texttt{eml} and the constant~$1$.
The gap is entirely on the \textsc{Phop} side and has three components:
(i)~that construction is a \emph{deep} composition---\texttt{oxieml}'s
$\mathrm{pow}(a,3/2)$ is a depth-$16$, $49$-node EML tree (it evaluates to
exactly $8$ at $a=4$), far beyond the depth-$\le\!4$ reach of our current
differentiable search; (ii)~our leaves are
restricted to a single variable or constant, with no \emph{affine} leaf
$a\,x_i+b$ (a parameterization choice, not an expressiveness one---affine
maps are themselves EML-expressible); and (iii)~the constructions are
valid on the complex/principal branch, where intermediate quantities such
as $\ln a$ for $a<1$ are negative, whereas our real-valued guarded
forward clamps the argument of $\ln$ to be positive. Conflating
``unreachable by our search'' with ``inexpressible in EML'' would
mis-state the theory; we are careful not to.

\paragraph{Future work.}
The diagnosis above points directly at the levers. \emph{Deeper and
warm-started search}: seeding the differentiable optimizer from the
\texttt{oxieml} constructive tree (or from a discrete enumeration) places
it in the correct basin---we already use enumeration warm-starts to
recover nested forms such as $\exp(\exp(x))$. \emph{A richer leaf basis}:
affine leaves expand the set of laws reachable at shallow depth without
leaving the EML grammar. \emph{Domain-aware evaluation} along the
principal branch would let the search follow the constructive identities
through negative intermediates. Beyond reachability, the ecosystem
substrate suggests several directions we find especially promising:
folding \texttt{scirs2-symbolic}'s \emph{dimensional analysis} into the
loss as a differentiable consistency penalty---bringing AI-Feynman's
most effective prior~\cite{udrescu2020aifeynman} into a gradient
framework; \emph{certified symbolic regression} as a first-class output
mode (existence, bounds, and equivalence proofs accompanying each law);
using the tensorized differentiable forest as the library-term generator
inside SINDy-style ODE/PDE discovery; and lowering discovered laws to
native CPU/GPU code for real-time deployment. The combination of a
single-operator basis, a tensorized differentiable population, and a
verifiable, deployable EML-IR ecosystem appears, to our knowledge, not to
be matched by any existing system.
